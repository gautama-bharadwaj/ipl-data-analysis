180 used to win games.

Every ball of every IPL match from 2008 to 2026: 295,000 deliveries across 1,243 matches, to find out what changed in IPL batting, when it changed, and why.

1 · The jump

Totals were flat for 15 years, then rose about 25 runs in four seasons

From 2008 to 2022 the average first-innings total stayed between 150 and 172. Then it rose to 183 in 2023, 190 in 2024, and close to 196 by 2026. 200 used to be an outlier. It is now roughly the median.

2 · When did it change?

The data points to one break, and it's 2023

We fit a simple "before vs after" model to the run rate of every full-length innings and tried every possible break season. The best fit is a break at 2023, with the run rate going from 8.1 to 9.5 runs per over. Earlier changes (2014, 2018, 2022) show up as small bumps, but none of them lasted.

3 · Where in the innings?

Every phase got faster, but the powerplay changed the most

Death overs have always been fast. The new part is the start of the innings: the powerplay went from about 7.8 runs an over in 2022 to over 10 in 2026. Teams no longer "build" for the first six overs.

Theory 1 · Pitches became more batter friendly

Batting really did get easier, not just riskier

If teams were only taking more risks, they would score faster but get out more often. Instead, runs per wicket went up too, from about 27 in 2020–22 to over 31 in 2026. Wickets in the powerplay barely moved, so the new ball isn't doing much less than before. Every major ground scored more after 2023, including slow ones like Chepauk.

Partly supported

Conditions do look easier: average and strike rate both rose, and at every ground. But the jump happened all at once in 2023 at every venue, which looks more like a rule change than pitches slowly changing. This data can't separate the surface from the batters playing on it.

Theory 2 · Boundary lines got shorter

Sixes went up everywhere, even at the biggest grounds

Cricsheet doesn't record boundary sizes, so this can't be measured directly. What we can see is where sixes come from. If ropes had moved in, you'd expect a jump at the grounds where it happened. Instead, sixes per match rose at every major ground at the same time, big or small. The sixes-to-fours ratio has been climbing steadily since 2014, not in one jump.

No rope-length data exists in this dataset. The pattern (sixes up at every ground, big or small, and a steady rise since 2014) fits better with harder hitting than with shorter boundaries. Shorter ropes may still add a little, but they don't explain the 2023 jump.

Theory 3 · A more attacking batting mindset

Batters now go hard from the first ball

The clearest change is in the first 10 balls of a batter's innings. Batters used to start slowly and accelerate later. From 2008 to 2022, the strike rate over a batter's first 10 balls was about 100–120. By 2026 it's 141, higher than most settled batters managed in the early years. The share of regular batters (100+ balls in a season) striking at 150 or more was never much above 1 in 5 before 2023. By 2026 it's 3 in 5.

Supported

The mindset shifted, and it's the main way the extra runs are scored. It had been building slowly since about 2014, then sped up sharply in 2023, the same year as the Impact Player rule. That timing suggests the rule is what gave batters licence to attack.

Theory 4 · The Impact Player rule

The rule changed behaviour more than the batting line-up

The rule lets a side add a specialist batter when chasing, or a bowler after batting first, so teams effectively bat one player deeper. Surprisingly, No. 8 doesn't come in to bat much more often than before, and from 2025 it happens less. What changed is how the top order bats knowing that cover is there: openers and No. 3 went from a strike rate of 133 in 2022 to 167 in 2026, and the biggest gains in run rate come when teams have lost three wickets or fewer.

Verdict

What changed, and when

When: one clear break in 2023, with smaller rises continuing through 2024–26. Before that, 2008–2022 was mostly flat with a few seasons that came and went (2014, 2018, 2022).

Where: every phase, but especially the powerplay, which went from about 7.8 to 10 runs an over.

How: more sixes (about 13 a match to over 19), fewer dot balls, and batters attacking from their first ball.

Why: the Impact Player rule is the most likely trigger, working through behaviour (insurance) rather than an extra batter actually batting. An attacking mindset, already building since about 2014, is how it shows up. Easier batting conditions are part of the picture, since averages rose as well. Boundary size can't be tested with this data, and the six data doesn't point to it.
